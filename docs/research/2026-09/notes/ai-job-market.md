# AI and Early-Career Software/IT Jobs (Global + India), 2024–2026: What the Evidence Says and What Makes Developers Hard to Replace

*Research notes compiled 2026-09-24. Every figure carries its date and source. Anything marked "(secondary)" was checked only in a news or aggregator write-up, not in the primary document. "Inferences" are my reasoning, not findings from the sources.*

## Q1. Early-career impact data (global, mostly US): Canaries paper, Harvard "seniority-biased" paper, NY Fed, Indeed Hiring Lab, 2026 junior-developer studies

### Takeaway
Three independent US data types (payroll, résumés, job postings) point the same way. Since late 2022, entry-level hiring into AI-exposed occupations such as software development has weakened. It shows up mostly as **fewer hires, not more firings**, and experienced workers are unaffected or growing. The evidence is **descriptive, US-centric and disputed** (see Q6). Also, US software postings have been **recovering since 2025**, but mostly for senior and AI-titled roles.

### Cited Findings

**Stanford Digital Economy Lab: "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen). Original Aug 2025; updated Aug 2026 with ADP payroll data through June 2026**
- Six facts from the Aug 2026 version: (1) no evidence of widespread, economy-wide job displacement; (2) employment of workers aged 22–25 in AI-exposed occupations "now stands 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with that of their less-exposed peers", with no comparable gap for experienced workers; (3) the gap "has widened steadily since we first documented it in August 2025"; (4) it "operates primarily through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations"; (5) declines are concentrated where AI usage substitutes for human tasks, while where usage complements workers "employment is flat or rising, especially for experienced workers"; (6) adjustment is happening through employment, not base pay. — [Stanford DEL, Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf); [Stanford DEL news, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)
- In levels: employment of 22–25-year-olds in the two most-exposed quintiles fell about 11% between Nov 2022 and June 2026. The same age group in the three least-exposed quintiles grew about 10%. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- How the headline number changed: earlier versions headlined a regression estimate of a **13% relative decline (July 2025 data)**, then **16% (Sept 2025 data)**. The 2026 version switches to a simpler descriptive "kept-pace shortfall": **15% at the July 2025 data vintage, 19% by June 2026**. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- Software developers specifically, from the 2025 versions: employment of software developers aged 22–25 had fallen **nearly 20% from its late-2022 peak**. The Aug 2026 version shows software developers only in appendix figures (Fig. B.3, H.1) and does not restate a single percentage in the text. — [Stanford DEL publication page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/); [Nov 2025 version PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2025/11/CanariesintheCoalMine_Nov25.pdf) (figure confirmed via search summary)
- **Automation vs augmentation.** The paper uses Anthropic Economic Index data on whether Claude usage for each occupation's tasks is "automative" (substituting) or "augmentative" (complementing). Occupations with the highest automation shares saw falling employment for the youngest workers. Occupations with the highest augmentation shares did not; "the fifth quintile has among the fastest employment growth." The age gradient ("automation associated with declines for the young, complementarity with gains for the experienced") is called "the paper's most direct evidence on mechanism." It holds for the March 2025 index release and for pooled Sept 2025, Jan 2026 and Apr 2026 releases. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- **Codified vs tacit knowledge.** Occupations that rely more on codified knowledge (formal, documented, textbook knowledge) have slower entry-level employment growth. Occupations that rely more on tacit knowledge (experience, on-the-job training) have faster employment growth for mid-career and senior workers. The codified gradient loses significance once college share is controlled for. The tacit gradient for experienced workers survives that control. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- Robustness: the young-worker divergence persists when excluding tech firms (NAICS 51, 5415) and computer occupations (SOC 15-1), when controlling for interest-rate exposure and remote-work amenability, and across five alternative AI-exposure measures. The authors say that excluding tech firms and computer occupations also "speaks against" the 2022 US Section 174 R&D-tax change as the explanation. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- Pay nuance for software developers: compensation for 22–25-year-old software developers grew somewhat faster than for older workers after ChatGPT, though the gap shrinks late in the sample. The authors suggest composition effects, for example firms cutting hiring of lower-paid engineers. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- Authors' own caveats: the results are "descriptive facts" and not causal estimates. The patterns attenuate when controlling for education and show some divergent trends that predate ChatGPT (around COVID). They are also stronger in the ADP sample than in national surveys: in the ACS, the 22–25 gap between most- and least-exposed quintiles is −0.022 with a CI that includes zero. The CPS has only 23–48 young software developers in the sample per month, too few to track reliably. — [Canaries Aug 2026 PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- A public tracker is maintained: [Stanford DEL Canaries Dashboard](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/indicators/canaries-dashboard/)

**Harvard: "Generative AI as Seniority-Biased Technological Change" (Hosseini Maasoum & Lichtinger). SSRN working paper; first version Aug 31, 2025; version reviewed dated Oct 27, 2025**
- Data: Revelio Labs résumé and job-posting data covering ~62 million US workers at ~285,000 firms (2015–2025) and ~200 million postings. A firm counts as a GenAI adopter if it posted a "GenAI integrator" role. 10,599 firms had adopted by March 2025: 3.7% of firms but 17.3% of employment. — [Oct 2025 version PDF (mirror)](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf); [SSRN 5425555](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5425555)
- From 2023Q1, junior employment at adopting firms fell **about 9% after six quarters** relative to non-adopters (difference-in-differences). The triple-difference estimate is **~10%** and the staggered event-study estimate is **~8% after eight quarters**. Senior employment showed "no sign of a break in trend." The earlier abstract reported **7.7% after six quarters**. — [Oct 2025 PDF](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf); [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5425555)
- Mechanism: the decline is "driven primarily by a substantial reduction in hiring." Junior separation rates actually fell, and promotions stayed stable. The decline is concentrated in high-GenAI-exposure roles, while low-exposure junior roles show "no significant change." — [Oct 2025 PDF](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf)
- College tier shows a **U-shaped pattern**: juniors from **mid-tier (tier 3–4) institutions** saw the steepest relative declines, while juniors from elite (tiers 1–2) and the lowest tier (5) saw smaller ones. — [Oct 2025 PDF](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf)
- The authors' interpretation (untested): firms may be making **forward-looking** hiring cuts for roles they expect to automate. "If firms have overreacted... the resulting decline in junior employment may be temporary." — [Oct 2025 PDF](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf)
- The paper quotes Fed Chair Powell at the Sept 2025 FOMC press conference: "You are seeing some effects... A particular focus on young people coming out of college... Hard to say how big it is." — [Oct 2025 PDF](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf)

**New York Fed: labor market for recent college graduates (ages 22–27)**
- Latest (2026:Q2): unemployment for recent graduates "stayed elevated at about 5.6 percent," and underemployment "edged up to 42 percent." — [NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market); Bloomberg reported 5.6% as of March 2026 — [Bloomberg, May 5, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-05/unemployment-for-recent-college-grads-remains-high-ny-fed-says)
- By major, Feb 2025 release (2023 data): computer engineering 7.5%, computer science 6.1%. — [EIG / Agglomerations, Aug 13, 2025](https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/a-viral-chart-on-recent-graduate)
- By major, Feb 2026 release (2024 data, 73 majors), secondary: computer engineering **7.5%** (2nd highest) and computer science **7.0%** (5th highest); anthropology was highest at 7.9%. The same majors had the **highest early-career wages**: computer engineering ~$90,000 and computer science ~$87,000, both with low underemployment. — [VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/tech-news/us-s-computer-engineering-grads-post-second-highest-unemployment-rate-5048192.html); [WRIC](https://www.wric.com/news/the-10-college-majors-with-the-highest-unemployment/); [Forbes, Feb 23, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaeltnietzel/2026/02/23/unemployment-and-underemployment-rates-among-recent-college-graduates/). I could not read the NY Fed's interactive table directly.
- Methodology critique (Connor O'Brien, EIG, Aug 13, 2025): the by-major estimates rest on small samples. The 95% CI for computer engineering is roughly **4%–11%**. On the broader "computer and information sciences" grouping, unemployment rose from under 4% (2022) to under 6% (2023), yet "a full 90 percent of 22- to 27-year-olds who majored in those fields were employed in 2023." — [EIG / Agglomerations](https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/a-viral-chart-on-recent-graduate)

**Indeed Hiring Lab: US software-development job postings**
- As of July 2026, US software development postings "remain about 27.5% below their pre-pandemic level" (Feb 2020), while overall postings are about at the Feb 2020 level. Software postings grew "almost 15%" after late Feb 2025, a period in which overall postings fell 7%. — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jul 8, 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Of the increase in software development postings between May 2025 and May 2026, **71% came from senior roles**, and **37% came from jobs with "AI" in the title**. — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jul 8, 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- The relationship flipped. Over May 2022–May 2026, "the more exposed to AI an occupation is, the more it declined". But "since 2025, AI-Exposed Occupations have been leading the Job Postings Recovery." Indeed cautions that "correlation does not imply causation." — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jul 8, 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- Seniority mix in Q1 2026: **69.3% of software development postings were senior-level and only 4.5% entry-level**, the most senior-skewed sector Indeed analysed. Per Indeed, between 2019 and 2025 mid-level postings fell 7.7 percentage points while senior postings grew ~9 points. Seniority is classified by algorithm (>99% coverage, ~95% accuracy on manual validation). Indeed notes that "sectors with high AI exposure and/or more opportunities to work remotely, like tech, tend to have fewer entry-level roles." — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jul 23, 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/)
- Series for ongoing tracking: [FRED: Software Development Job Postings on Indeed (US)](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/IHLIDXUSTPSOFTDEVE)

**Other 2025–2026 evidence on junior developers**
- PwC 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer (June 15, 2026; includes 2.4 million US entry-level job ads): AI-exposed entry-level roles are **7x more likely to demand senior-level skills** such as leadership, creativity and judgment. These roles **grew 35% since 2019**, while **other entry-level roles declined 10%**. — [PwC via PR Newswire, Jun 15, 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-reshapes-global-labour-market-into-two-distinct-paths-rewarding-human-skills-pwc-2026-global-ai-jobs-barometer-302798987.html)
- Anthropic Economic Index, software development (Apr 28, 2025): **79%** of Claude Code (agentic tool) conversations were classified as automation, against 49% on Claude.ai. The most common languages were JavaScript/TypeScript (31%) and HTML/CSS (28%), and the top use case was UI/UX component development (12%). Anthropic suggests "jobs that center on making simple applications and user interfaces might face earlier disruption." — [Anthropic, Apr 28, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/impact-software-development)
- Yu & Moon (arXiv, submitted Jul 19, 2026; qualitative, 14 interviews in South Korea): GenAI "redirects entry-level work into senior-AI workflows," so juniors miss formative learning tasks. The authors call for "deliberate institutional design across classrooms, workplaces, and the evaluation criteria for juniors." — [arXiv 2607.17067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17067)

### Inferences
- For a fresh graduate, the evidence says the pressure is **at the hiring door, not mass firing of juniors already employed**. In both Canaries and the Harvard study, separations did not rise; Harvard even found junior separations fell. Once someone is inside and building tacit, on-the-job knowledge, the data show less risk.
- The work being substituted is **codified, checkable, routine work**: boilerplate code, simple UIs, well-specified tickets. That has historically been the classic "junior ticket." Judgment built through experience is complemented. So the implied strategy is to **build tacit and judgment skills faster** (debugging real systems, owning features end to end, domain context) rather than compete with AI on raw code output.
- The US software postings rebound is real but tilted toward **senior and AI-titled roles**. A junior who can work "like a strong mid-level with AI" and knows AI-application tooling is best aligned with where demand is recovering.
- All of this is US data. India's services-heavy market behaves differently (Q2), so these percentages should not be transplanted onto India.

### Gaps
- The Aug 2026 Canaries version gives no text-stated percentage for 22–25-year-old software developers; the "nearly 20%" figure comes from the 2025 versions and I confirmed it only via a search summary.
- I could not read the NY Fed by-major table directly. The Feb 2026 (2024 data) figures come from secondary reporting, and they carry wide confidence intervals.
- One search summary said ~47% of US software development postings mentioned AI as of April 2026 (Indeed). I did not verify this in the fetched Indeed text, so it is excluded above.
- I found no payroll-based study of junior developer hiring for India comparable to Canaries or Harvard.

## Q2. India IT: layoffs and restructuring 2025–2026, fresher/campus hiring, AI talent demand vs supply, AI salary premiums, GCC AI hiring

### Takeaway
Indian IT is **restructuring rather than collapsing**. The top five services firms' combined headcount dipped slightly in FY26, driven by TCS (−23,460). NASSCOM reports only ~2.3% net tech-job growth as AI decouples revenue from headcount. Fresher hiring continues but is **more selective**: IT fresher hiring intent fell to 76%, and employers now expect AI fluency plus project evidence. AI/ML hiring is growing fast (+25% to +45% YoY on Naukri), but most strongly in **high-salary and experienced bands**. GCCs (2,117 centres, 2.36M staff) are an increasingly important AI employer.

The same report says clients were seeking 20–30% price reductions in new contracts. — [Gulf News, Jul 2025](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/ai-overhaul-indias-tcs-to-cut-12000-jobs-this-year-after-growth-slows-in-q1-1.500212931)

## Q4. Productivity evidence: METR RCT (2025) and 2026 update, GitHub Copilot experiment, Microsoft/Accenture field experiments, the Anthropic skill-formation RCT, and what they imply for a junior developer

### Takeaway
Controlled studies disagree because they measure different things. On **well-specified tasks**, AI speeds developers up a lot (55.8% faster in the Copilot lab task; 26% more completed tasks in field RCTs), and **less-experienced developers gain the most**. For **experts in large, familiar codebases**, early-2025 tools **slowed them down by 19%** even though they believed they were faster. For **learners**, delegating to AI **cut comprehension and debugging skill** (quiz 50% vs 67%). For a junior, AI is a real accelerator but a **risk to skill formation** unless it is used deliberately.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub Copilot controlled experiment** (Peng, Kalliamvakou, Cihon, Demirer; submitted Feb 13, 2023): developers were asked to implement an HTTP server in JavaScript. "The treatment group, with access to the AI pair programmer, completed the task **55.8% faster** than the control group." The authors note heterogeneous effects that "show promise for AI pair programmers to help people transition into software development careers." It is a single, greenfield lab task. — [arXiv 2302.06590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590)
- **Three field RCTs at Microsoft, Accenture and an anonymous Fortune 100 firm** (Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng, Salz; SSRN 2024, later published in *Management Science*): across **4,867 developers**, Copilot access produced a **26.08% increase in completed tasks**. **Less experienced developers adopted it more and gained more.** Per MIT Sloan's summary, gains were **27–39% for junior developers vs 8–13% for senior developers**. Adoption was ~60% after a year. The researchers **could not assess code quality**. — [SSRN 4945566](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4945566); [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535); [MIT Sloan, Nov 4, 2024](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/how-generative-ai-affects-highly-skilled-workers)
- **METR RCT** (published Jul 10, 2025): **16 experienced open-source developers** did **246 real tasks** (~2 hours each) on large, mature repositories averaging 22k+ GitHub stars and more than 1M lines of code. They used Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet. With AI allowed, **tasks took 19% longer** (CI +2% to +39%). Beforehand, developers **expected a 24% speedup**; afterwards they **still believed AI had sped them up by 20%**. METR says the result does not show that AI fails to speed up most developers, and that it may not apply to **less experienced developers or unfamiliar codebases**. — [METR, Jul 10, 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/); [METR 2026 update restating CI](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- **METR update** (Feb 24, 2026): a late-2025 follow-up ran with **57 developers and 800+ tasks**. METR reports an estimated "speedup" of **−18% (CI −38% to +9%)** for returning developers and **−4% (CI −15% to +9%)** for newly recruited ones. These are changes in task time relative to the earlier +19%, so they point toward AI now saving time, and "our raw results show some evidence for speedup." Both intervals include zero. METR calls the estimates **unreliable because of selection effects**: many developers refused to work without AI even at $50/hr (pay had fallen from $150/hr), and "30% to 50% of developers" withheld tasks they did not want to do without AI. METR suggests the true speedup could be higher among the developers and tasks that dropped out, and it is changing its study design. — [METR, Feb 24, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- **Anthropic skill-formation RCT** (Jan 29, 2026): **52 mostly junior engineers** learned Trio, an unfamiliar async Python library. On a quiz taken right afterwards, the **AI-assisted group scored 50% vs 67%** for those who coded by hand (Cohen's d = 0.738, p = 0.01; "nearly two letter grades"). The **largest gap was on debugging questions**. The AI group finished ~2 minutes faster, which was not statistically significant. Interaction style mattered. Pure delegation, progressive reliance and "iterative AI debugging" averaged **below 40%**. "Generation-then-comprehension," hybrid code-plus-explanation and **conceptual inquiry** averaged **65%+**, and conceptual inquiry was the fastest of the high-scoring patterns. Caveats: small sample and an immediate test. — [Anthropic, Jan 29, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)
- The Stack Overflow 2025 frustrations (66% "almost right," 45.2% "debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming") and DORA 2025's "higher throughput but also higher instability" point to hidden verification costs. — [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai); [DORA 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)
- Labor-market-level productivity is small so far in one well-measured country. In Denmark (surveys of 25,000 workers across 11 exposed occupations, linked to employer-employee registers, 2023–2024), AI chatbots saved ~3% of work time on average and had **precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours**, ruling out effects larger than ~1–2%. — [Humlum & Vestergaard, NBER w33777](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33777/revisions/w33777.rev0.pdf); [BFI working paper](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-1.pdf) (details via search summary)

### Inferences
- **What this means for a junior.** (1) AI can make a junior's measurable output grow faster than a senior's on routine tasks. That is an argument for adopting AI tools early, and it matches AWS CEO Matt Garman's point (Q5) that juniors are often the most AI-fluent. (2) If the junior **delegates instead of understanding**, AI erodes exactly the **debugging and code-reading skills** needed to review AI output, which is the skill employers now rank highest. (3) Self-perception is unreliable (METR: people who were 19% slower felt 20% faster), so actual time and defect rates are worth measuring rather than trusting "it feels faster."
- A learning path that fits this evidence: use AI for **explanations, conceptual questions and review**; write and debug some code **without AI** on purpose (as Microsoft's "Frontier Professionals" do); and always read, test and explain AI-generated code before accepting it.
- Because the field RCTs show juniors' throughput rising, firms need **fewer juniors per unit of routine work**, which fits NASSCOM's point that headcount is rising more slowly than revenue. Juniors are therefore likely to compete on **judgment, verification, domain understanding and ownership**, not on typing speed.

### Gaps
- There are no large RCTs on **agentic tools** (Claude Code, Cursor agents, Codex-style agents) with junior developers in real enterprise or IT-services settings, and none measuring **code quality or maintenance cost** over time.
- METR's 2026 estimate is explicitly unreliable, so there is no clean current estimate of the speedup for experienced developers.
- I did not verify the Copilot RCT sample size (reported elsewhere as ~95 recruited developers), so it is omitted.
- I found no India-specific RCTs, for example inside Indian IT services firms.

## Q5. What makes a developer hard to replace in the AI era; roles that are growing; evidence that "AI-exposed but augmented" work is growing

### Takeaway
The evidence is mostly correlational, but it points one way. AI **substitutes for codified, routine, easily checked coding work**, such as boilerplate and simple UI and CRUD tasks, and **complements tacit, experience-based judgment**. Developers are hardest to replace when they (a) can **verify, debug and own** AI-generated output, (b) understand **systems and the business domain** well enough to frame problems, and (c) can **build with AI**: LLM apps, RAG, agents, evaluation, MLOps/LLMOps, and the data and cloud plumbing behind them. The fastest-growing roles are AI engineer, AI/ML and data specialist, cybersecurity, and "professionalised" roles where AI raises the value of human judgment.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence that augmentation (rather than automation) goes with job growth**
- Occupations where AI usage is mainly **augmentative** show flat or rising employment, including for 22–25-year-olds (the top augmentation quintile is "among the fastest employment growth"). Jobs with AI in the title account for **37%** of the May 2025–May 2026 increase in software development postings. — [Indeed Hiring Lab, Jul 8, 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- "Professionalised" roles, where AI handles the routine and human judgment is emphasized, saw **2x the job growth and 42% faster salary growth** than "democratised" roles. **Skills that research and employer surveys point to**
- **Verifying AI output and critical thinking**: "quality control of AI output" (50%) and "critical thinking" (46%) are rated the top human skills as AI takes on more work — [Microsoft Work Trend Index, May 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization). **Analytical thinking** is the top core skill, and **quality control** differentiates growing jobs from declining ones — [WEF FoJ 2025](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf). 66% of developers are frustrated by "almost right" AI output, and 75% turn to a person when they don't trust the AI — [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai).

Learners who used AI for **conceptual questions and explanations** kept their learning (65%+ quiz scores vs under 40% for delegators). — [Anthropic RCT, Jan 29, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)
- **Keeping skills sharp on purpose**: high-impact "Frontier Professionals" are more likely to do some work without AI deliberately (43% vs 30%). — [Microsoft WTI, May 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)
- **AI engineering stack**. ### Inferences
*My reasoning for the learning path; not findings from the sources.*
- **Priority 1: fundamentals as "verification capital."** Reading unfamiliar code, debugging, testing, Git, SQL, HTTP/APIs, and basic OS and networking. The evidence (Anthropic RCT, Stack Overflow's "almost right" problem, Microsoft's top skill being quality control of AI output) says the scarce human role is judging and fixing AI output, and that takes real understanding.
- **Priority 2: build with AI in Python.** LLM APIs, RAG, tool use and agents, **evaluation of LLM outputs**, and basic LLMOps/MLOps (deploying, monitoring, cost, guardrails). This maps directly to LinkedIn's AI Engineer skills (LangChain, RAG, PyTorch, MLOps) and TeamLease's in-demand list (orchestration, agent design, LLM safety). His Java background also fits enterprise backends in IT services and GCCs, where AI features get integrated.
- **Priority 3: system design, cloud and data engineering.** AI apps are mostly plumbing: data pipelines, APIs, reliability. DORA says AI increases instability, so reliability skills are a complement.
- **Priority 4: security basics, including AI-specific risks** such as prompt injection and data leakage. Cyber demand is rising (WEF's second-fastest-growing skill; Naukri +32%). I did not find a clean data series for "AI security" as its own job category.
- **Priority 5: domain knowledge and problem framing** in a client vertical (BFSI, retail, healthcare, telecom), plus **communication and ownership**. PwC's growing "professionalised" roles and the Canaries tacit-knowledge finding both reward context and judgment that AI does not have.
- **Priority 6: a deliberate AI-augmented workflow.** Use AI for explanations and conceptual questions, do periodic no-AI practice, always test and explain accepted code, and measure your own speed and defects.
- **Priority 7: visible proof.** Hands-on projects, internships and hackathons, because Indian employers explicitly screen for "hands-on project experience from day one."
- Avoid building a career mainly on **simple UI or CRUD coding**, the category Anthropic flags as most exposed to agentic automation. That does not mean avoiding front-end work entirely; it means moving toward system and product ownership.

### Gaps
- Almost all "which skills protect you" evidence is **correlational** (postings, surveys, adoption data). I found no causal study showing that a given individual skill improves retention or promotion odds for junior developers.
- I found no reliable India data series on **AI security**, **LLMOps/AI platform**, or **forward-deployed engineer** jobs as separate categories. The FDE figures come from vendor blogs.
- I found no evidence on whether IT-services freshers who upskill in AI get measurably better project allocation, retention or pay. That would be the ideal evidence for this learning path.

## Q6. What we don't know: uncertainty and conflicting findings

### Takeaway
US payroll data show real early-career weakness in AI-exposed jobs, but several things remain unresolved. **It is unclear whether AI is the main cause, how large the effect is, whether it applies outside the US (including India), whether it is temporary, and how quickly agentic tools will reach higher-skill work.** Credible researchers disagree, and the honest position is "early signal, not settled fact."

### Cited Findings
- **Causality is not established.** The Canaries authors call their results "descriptive facts" and say that "ongoing work is needed to determine how much they are caused by the spread of generative AI rather than merely correlated with it." The estimates **attenuate when controlling for education**, show some **pre-ChatGPT divergence**, and are **stronger in ADP payroll data than in national surveys**; the ACS gap for 22–25-year-olds is −0.022 with a CI that includes zero. — [Stanford DEL Canaries, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)
- **Contrary evidence from the Economic Innovation Group (EIG).** Using CPS data (Aug 2025), Eckhardt & Goldschlag found **no detectable difference in unemployment** between more- and less-AI-exposed workers on three of five exposure measures, including their preferred one. The other two showed only 0.2–0.3 percentage-point differences. — [EIG, "AI and Jobs: The Final Word (Until the Next One)", Aug 2025](https://eig.org/ai-and-jobs-the-final-word/) (via search summary). In a follow-up (Mar 5, 2026), Ozimek & Goldschlag argue that youth labor-market weakness is **broad**: young people **without** degrees did marginally worse than graduates. In their view, the "real mystery" is general youth weakness, and AI is likely not the primary driver. — [EIG, Mar 5, 2026](https://eig.org/ai-and-young-adult-jobs-the-real-mystery/)
- **Yale Budget Lab.** The occupational mix "is not yet changing in ways that clearly align with the introduction of AI," and AI-usage measures show "no connection to changes in employment or unemployment." The early-2026 labor market has "low layoffs but also low hiring." There is a "nascent signal" in how young workers' occupations differ from older workers', but samples are small and it may predate ChatGPT. — [Yale Budget Lab, "Evaluating the Impact of AI on the Labor Market"](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/evaluating-impact-ai-labor-market-current-state-affairs); [Yale Budget Lab, "What We Do and Don't Know..."](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/what-we-do-and-dont-know-about-how-ai-affecting-labor-market) (via search summaries; the page fetch failed)
- **Null effects in Denmark.** In 11 AI-exposed occupations, chatbots had precise null effects on earnings and hours two years after adoption. — [Humlum & Vestergaard, NBER w33777](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33777/revisions/w33777.rev0.pdf)
- **Competing explanations** for weak junior tech hiring: the 2022 interest-rate tightening, the post-COVID correction to tech over-hiring, the US Section 174 R&D-tax change, offshoring, and economic uncertainty. Both Canaries and the Harvard paper test several of these (for example, excluding tech firms, adding industry-by-seniority-by-time fixed effects, checking pre-trends) and still find the pattern. Neither claims to rule them out definitively. — [Canaries Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf); [Harvard, Oct 2025](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf)
- **Temporary or permanent?** Harvard's hypothesis is that firms cut junior hiring pre-emptively, expecting automation. If firms overreacted, "the resulting decline in junior employment may be temporary." — [Harvard, Oct 2025](https://www.alejandrobarros.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ssrn-5425555.pdf). US software postings have been rebounding since 2025 (+~15%), though they remain ~27.5% below Feb 2020 and are skewed senior. — [Indeed, Jul 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/)
- **Noisy headline statistics.** The widely shared NY Fed "CS/CE unemployment" figures come from small samples; the computer engineering estimate has a 95% CI of roughly 4%–11%. — [EIG / Agglomerations, Aug 2025](https://agglomerations.eig.org/p/a-viral-chart-on-recent-graduate)
- **Productivity evidence is unsettled.** METR's 2025 slowdown (+19% time) versus its possible late-2025 speedup (−18%, CI −38% to +9%) is confounded by selection bias. The field RCTs did not measure code quality. DORA finds AI raises instability. — [METR, Feb 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/how-generative-ai-affects-highly-skilled-workers); [DORA 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)
- **Capabilities are changing fast.** AI performance on software-engineering benchmarks rose from **4.4% to 71.7%** between 2023 and 2024 (Stanford AI Index, as cited in Canaries), and agentic coding use leans heavily toward automation (79% on Claude Code in Apr 2025). How far up the skill ladder substitution will reach is unknown. — [Canaries Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf); [Anthropic, Apr 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/impact-software-development)
- **India-specific uncertainty.** India's slowdown overlaps with a demand and pricing squeeze, not only AI: TCS's Q1 FY26 revenue fell 3.1% in constant currency, and clients sought 20–30% price cuts. IT hiring on Naukri went from flat in FY26 and −3% in June 2026 to +6% in July and +11% in August 2026, which could be cyclical. — [Gulf News, Jul 2025](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/ai-overhaul-indias-tcs-to-cut-12000-jobs-this-year-after-growth-slows-in-q1-1.500212931); [Naukri Mar 2026](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-march-26-records-a-9-rise-in-white-collar-hiring-as-fy26-closes-at-8-the-strongest-job-growth-in-three-years/); [Naukri Jun 2026](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-white-collar-hiring-grows-6-in-june-2026-ai-ml-and-fresher-hiring-lead-the-charge/); [LatestLY/ANI, Sep 2026](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/business-news-aiml-hiring-rises-31-pc-yoy-in-august-gcc-recruitment-grows-10-pc-naukri-jobspeak-7594785.html). India AI-talent projections disagree because they use different definitions: Bain (2.3M openings vs 1.2M talent by 2027), Deloitte–NASSCOM (1.25M talent pool by 2027), TeamLease Digital (53% gap by 2026). — [Gulf News (Bain)](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/indias-ai-sector-poised-to-surpass-23-million-job-openings-by-2027-1.500056470); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/bridging-the-ai-talent-gap-to-boost-indias-tech-and-economic-impact-deloitte-nasscom-report.html); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/indias-ai-market-grows-but-talent-gap-expected-to-reach/496465)

### Inferences
- **A calm, accurate framing for the graduate:** "The entry door into routine coding work has narrowed, especially in the US data. There is no evidence of mass displacement of developers. Experienced and AI-augmented roles are growing, AI/ML hiring in India is up ~25–45% YoY, and Indian IT is still hiring freshers, with a higher bar." That framing does not require believing either the doom or the hype.
- Because the uncertainty runs both ways (the effects could grow as agents improve, or partly reverse if firms overreacted or the cycle turns), the robust choice is a skill set that pays off either way: fundamentals and verification, building with AI, systems/data/cloud, domain knowledge, and communication.
- Headlines that cite a single number (for example "CS unemployment 7%" or "AI will take X% of jobs") should be read against sample sizes, what is being measured (postings, payroll or surveys), and the date.

### Gaps
- There is **no India-specific, payroll-based or causal study** of AI's effect on fresher or junior developer employment. India evidence consists of company disclosures, job-postings indices (Naukri), employer-intent surveys (TeamLease) and industry projections (NASSCOM, Bain, Deloitte).
- There is no credible forecast of how many **Indian IT-services** roles AI will displace or create by 2030. WEF's figures are global and cover all occupations.
- It is unknown how the shift to **outcome-based pricing** (NASSCOM) will change fresher intake ratios in 2027–2030.
- Yale Budget Lab and EIG details were read only through search summaries (page fetches failed).
